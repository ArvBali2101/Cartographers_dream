# MAP — research and adaptation notes

Research reviewed 27 September 2026. This is an original adaptation of motifs, not a claim that all of its rules exist in Lovecraft's fiction. The cartographic invitations, signatures, witnessing seals and unnamed figure are original game lore.

## Primary literary sources

- In [The Call of Cthulhu](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/cc.aspx), investigators connect disturbing dreams, an image and accounts of the sunken city R'lyeh. Its monumental architecture violates ordinary spatial expectations. Adaptation: the drowned chart, shared dream evidence, submerged monoliths and a threat too large to see in full. Cthulhu is a literary inspiration, not a fully modelled boss here.
- [Nyarlathotep](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/n.aspx) links an enigmatic demonstrator, unsettling exhibitions, nightmares and the breakdown of a familiar city. Adaptation: the city exhibition and a host who knows the expedition. The host is not conclusively identified as Nyarlathotep; the small figure remains unnamed.
- [The Dreams in the Witch House](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/dwh.aspx) joins unusual angles, mathematics, dream travel and experiences crossing the boundary of waking life. Adaptation: the observatory bearings, displaced doors and walls that look geometrically wrong. Mapping as a supernatural connection is our invention, not a quotation or established rule from that story.
- In [The Nameless City](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/nc.aspx), a descent and a sequence of murals gradually reveal a history that does not fit the explorer's human assumptions. Adaptation: four distinct cave paintings, increasing clarity through observation and ancient architecture below a seemingly primitive entrance. The game does not import the story's cultural stereotypes.
- [The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/dq.aspx) supplies a dream journey, deceptive guidance and Nyarlathotep's role. Adaptation: a connected journey through changing environments and guidance that is useful without being trustworthy. The game's Blind God is original and is not presented as a faithful depiction of Azathoth.

## Folklore and material evidence

[Historic England's account of Creswell Crags](https://historicengland.org.uk/whats-new/news/witches-marks-discovery-largest-in-britain/) discusses marks interpreted as protective, including lines, boxes and mazes thought to entrap evil. These interpretations are not proof of supernatural effects or a universal explanation of every scratch. Adaptation: deliberately different protective rosettes on thresholds and red witnessing eyes to erase. Protective marks should never look identical to the seals the player must destroy.

The [National Library of Wales account of corpse-candle traditions](https://www.library.wales/news/article/one-dark-night-tales-of-the-cannwyll-corff) distinguishes death-portending lights from other British ghost-light stories in which travellers may be helped or misled. Adaptation: drifting wisps whose presence is atmospheric, not a guarantee of a safe route. These Welsh traditions are not labelled ancient jungle beliefs or Neolithic evidence.

## Submission-facing design decisions

- Explain the player's situation in short chapter conclusions; keep optional archive entries for deeper Mythos context.
- Let each additional objective change the player's route. Bigger scenery alone does not make a longer level.
- Use distant movement, a timed submerged eye, responsive sound, lighting interruption and a post-seal pursuit surge instead of constant loud jumpscares.
- Retain stable seeded geography for speedrunning. Required routes, gates and deadlines require automated checks and eventually a human timed playtest.
- Avoid presenting a win, a particular playtime, an exact other-device frame rate or full production polish as verified facts.

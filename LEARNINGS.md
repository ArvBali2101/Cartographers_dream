# DreamScape learnings

- The product is more memorable when it is framed as a persistent world that remembers dreams, not an AI dream interpreter.
- The LLM should be treated as a structured extraction layer; matching, recurrence, route ordering, and world placement should remain deterministic application logic.
- The strongest prototype surface is the map itself: recurring places, dream signs, routes, fog, and replay should be visible before analytics.
- Synthetic interconnected dreams are safer and more controllable for a live demo; research corpora are better positioned as validation data.
- DreamScape should describe associations found in a user’s journal and avoid psychological claims or pseudo-interpretation.
- A single-screen working surface is a good hackathon MVP: journal input, map, and insights are visible together so the value is immediately legible.
- Three product concepts need three different cinematic treatments: one dream as a complete scene, all dreams as a continuously expanding world, and the most recurring dream as a replayable composite scene.
- The best visual hierarchy is movie scene first, memory/map system underneath. Generated artwork creates immersion while deterministic overlays preserve interaction and credibility.
- A landmark must behave like a portal in a dream game: selecting Rooftop should move the user into Rooftop, not merely open a metadata card. Memory details can remain available, but scene transition is the primary interaction.
